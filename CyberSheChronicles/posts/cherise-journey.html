Member Spotlight: Cherise's Journey into Cybersecurity

January, 2026 | Cherise Osambo | Member Spotlight

About the Author

Cherise Osambo is a cybersecurity enthusiast and an alumni of the african-girls-can-code-initiative-agcci and SiemensEmpowerHer Program. She's passionate about creating an ecosystem and a space where the next generation and current generation of women in tech can collaborate,innovate and network.

From Zero to Cybersecurity Hero

While I had some tech exposure, I never imagined I could build a career in it—until I found CyberShe. This ecosystem showed me that cybersecurity isn't about knowing everything; it's about having a space to collaborate, learn, and grow. Through AGCCI and SiemensEmpowerHer, I discovered that in cybersecurity, we don't just use technology—we innovate, protect, and create safer spaces for everyone.

The Gap I Experienced

As someone with tech background but limited cybersecurity exposure, I realized something was missing. I could navigate systems, but I didn't understand their vulnerabilities. More importantly, I noticed how few women were in security roles—not because they lacked capability, but because they lacked access and community.

Building What Was Needed

That's when CyberShe was born. Not as just another training program, but as an ecosystem. I designed it around three pillars:

  • Accessible Entry: Starting with "What is a firewall?" not assuming prior knowledge
  • Practical Application: Real-world simulations from day one
  • Community Support: Regular masterclasses where everyone's questions were valid

The breakthrough came from my journey with AGCCI and SiemensEmpowerHer. From it, I created something radical: a pathway that didn't just teach tech, but built the collaborative network women need to thrive in it.

The CyberShe Difference

What makes our ecosystem work isn't fancy technology—it's human connection. We match beginners with mentors who've walked the same path. We turn theoretical knowledge into community projects.We measure success not just by certificates earned, but by networks built and confidence gained.

Community Makes the Difference

What truly made my journey possible was the SiemensEmpowerHer community. When I struggled with subnetting (and I struggled a lot!), there was always someone available to help. We shared resources,we interacted with mentors during masterclasses, we move together during networking events,we shared tech events and opportunities like expos and hackathons and celebrated each small victory together.

One memorable moment was during our "Night of Code" event. Paired with a more experienced member, we built a simple network monitoring tool. Seeing my code actually work was exhilarating—it was the moment I truly believed I could do this.

Where I Am Today

Today, I wear multiple hats that all serve one mission: creating space for women in cybersecurity. As a student of Information Security and Forensics, I'm continuously learning. As a SiemensEmpowerHer mentor, I'm paying forward the guidance I received. As a leader at Girls on Chain, I'm expanding access to blockchain technology. And as founder of CyberShe, I'm building the ecosystem I wish existed when I started—one where every woman knows she belongs in tech.

My advice to anyone considering this path:

  1. Start where you are: You don't need to know everything before you begin.
  2. Embrace the community: You don't have to do this alone.
  3. Celebrate small wins: Every concept mastered is progress.
  4. Keep showing up: Consistency beats intensity every time.

Cybersecurity needs diverse perspectives. Your unique background—whether it's hospitality, education, arts, or anything else—brings valuable problem-solving skills to the table. The tech world needs more voices like yours.
